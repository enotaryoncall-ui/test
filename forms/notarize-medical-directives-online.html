Medical Directive Forms You Can Notarize Online

Choose from a range of healthcare planning documents and get them notarized quickly with a trusted Online Notary service.

Advance Healthcare Directive

States your medical treatment preferences in advance so your wishes are honored if you're unable to communicate them yourself.

Living Will

Documents your end-of-life care preferences, including life-sustaining treatment decisions, for your medical team and family.

Medical Power of Attorney

Appoints a trusted person to make healthcare decisions on your behalf if you become unable to make them yourself.

Healthcare Proxy

Designates a healthcare agent to communicate and enforce your medical wishes directly with providers.

HIPAA Release Authorization

Authorizes specific individuals to access your protected health information and communicate with your healthcare providers.

Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Acknowledgment

Formalizes your directive regarding resuscitation efforts in the event of cardiac or respiratory arrest.

Organ Donation Consent

Formally documents your wishes regarding organ and tissue donation for your medical records and family.

Mental Health Treatment Declaration

Outlines your preferences for mental health treatment in advance, in the event you're unable to make those decisions later.

A medical directive, also called an advance healthcare directive, is a legal document that states your healthcare preferences and appoints someone to make medical decisions on your behalf if you become unable to do so yourself.
Commonly notarized medical directive documents include:
  • Advance Healthcare Directives
  • Living Wills
  • Medical Power of Attorney
  • Healthcare Proxy designations
  • HIPAA release authorizations
  • Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) acknowledgments
Requirements vary by state.
Many states accept notarization as a valid way to execute a medical directive, while some also require or allow witness signatures instead of or in addition to notarization. Check your state's specific requirements before submitting your document.
Some states require one or more witnesses along with notarization for a medical directive to be valid. Review your state's requirements or consult an attorney to confirm what your specific document needs.
